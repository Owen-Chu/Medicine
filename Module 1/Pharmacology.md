# Pharmacodynamics
* Drug action in humans
* Drug acts on receptor or physiological mechanisms
* Leads to measurable change in body
* Different drug response in individuals

## Sites
* **Receptors** - number available, configuration and binding of drug, physiological processes triggered by drug binding
* **Enzymes** - intrinsic activity of enzyme, drug binding
* **Ion channels** - number, conformation of channel

**Agonist** - Drug and receptor, Activation, Cellular response
**Antagonist** - Drug and receptor, Binding, No response

# Pharmacokinetics (drug movement)
* Drug enter body and travels to site of action
* Factors affecting drug concentration: Bioavailability (absorption from stomach), First-pass metabolism (breakdown by liver enzymes), Clearance (metabolism and elimination through urine)

## Bioavailability
* Proportion of drug that gets into systemic circulation
* **Oral**: How much can be absorbed, First-pass metabolism
* Calculated by comparing amount after oral dose and amount after iv dose

## Half life
* Time taken for drug concentration to halve
* Determinant of dosing regimen (how much and how often)
* How long the drug is in body

## Importance
* Drugs need to be administered in an acceptable and feasible way (oral, injection, rectal, topical)
* Substance has to traverse the body, active compound has to reach target site, remain at target for enough time at an appropriate concentration and eliminated from body in a consistent and predictable way.
* Target drug concentration delivers good efficacy but not toxicity.

# Licensing approval
* Satisfactory Physico-chemical properties
* Good manufacturing practice
* Stability
* Pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics
* **Clinical trials** - efficacy and toxicity
* Acceptable packaging/marketing and patient information
* Monitoring presciption

## Categories
* **Prescription Only Medicines** (**POM**) - Sold/supplied only on a prescription
* **Pharmacy medicines** (**P**) - Sold/supplied only from pharmacist
* **General Sales List** (**GSL**) - Sold/supplied direct to public unopened from lockable premises

## Unlicensed prescribing
* Drug not licensed for patient group or disease
* Exercise duty of care
* Indication is not licensed
* Determine efficacy/safety is proven elsewhere
* Explain license status to patient
* **CONSENT!**
* Responsibility lies on clinician

## Licensing
* Safe
* Effective
* Adequate quality control
* $\neq$ drug is better (only superior over placebo or no worse than established agent)
* Prescriber still needs to prioritise choices within treatment pathways.

## Benefit harm balance
* Right drug, right dose at right time for the right patient
* Clinical trials - 'average effect'
* Some patients benefit sometimes
* Sometimes harm, varying severity
* Conditions can now have prediction calculators/prognostic scoring
* Choose drugs with higher chance of benefit
* Avoid high risk of harm drugs

## Adverse reactions
* Dose - greater risk as dose increases due to pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic relationship
* Time/duration - cumulative dose over months or frequency of use
* Individual susceptibility factors - age, physiological impairment, genetic susceptibility
* Reduce harm - evidence-based choice of relevant drug for patient, avoid high risk drugs, adjust dose/duration, monitor to detect problems (hypersensitivity/allergies difficult to predict with early doses)
