# LO: Describe the methodology and interpretation of clinical biochemistry laboratory tests

[[Clinical biochemistry]] - Analysis of bodily fluids/samples
* Make or confirm a diagnosis and monitoring of treatment
* Assessment of the progression of disease
* Screening for disease
* Research into biochemical basis of disease and clinical trails of new drugs

## Information we want
* Is it present, or an outside-of-normal-range quantity. (qualitative, yes/no)
* Is concentration 'abnormal' (quantitative)
* What is the concentration (quantitative)
* **Qualitative** - lateral flow test (pregnancy test, COVID test)
* **Semi-quantitative** - Colorimetric (Urinary dipstick)

## Types of sample
* Blood
* Urine
* Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF)
* Saliva
* Fecal
* Sweat
* Stones

Biological samples are a complex mix of:
* Water
* Ions
* Proteins
* Lipids
* Hormones
* Small metabolites
* Cells

### Blood
* **Whole blood** - plasma, red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets
* **Serum** - clear liquid separated from clotted blood
* **Plasma** - liquid portion of blood, obtained by centrifugation and treated with anticoagulant

Most blood samples need to be centrifuged or processed

### Urine
* **Spot**: Collected at any time
* **First morning**: More concentrated, used for pregnancy tests and microalbumin
* **Timed collection**: Measure specific analytes

### Saliva
* Water, mucus, proteins, salts and amylase
* food debris, bacteria, white blood cells
* Spit in tube or salivette

### Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)
* Glucose, proteins, lipids, electrolytes, white blood cells
* Lumbar puncture

### Faeces
* Foecal Immunulogical test (FIT) tube
* Plain tube

## Collection and preparation
* **Appropriate collection technique** - Venepuncture, order of draw
* Appropriate collection device/tube for test
* **Process sample** - Mixing/centrifugation
* **Transport** - Temperature and stability of analyte, light, freeze/thaw cycles

## Analytical methods
* [[Spectrophotometry]]
* [[Electrophoresis]]
* [[Chromatography]]
* [[Immuno assays]]

### Spectrophotometry
* Measures absorbance of light by solution
* range of underlying methods
* Wide range of analytes

Beer-Lambert Law: A = $\epsilon$bc
Absorbance (A) = molar absorptivity ($\epsilon$) * path length (b) * concentration (c)

### Electrophoresis
* Separation of protein, RNA, DNA
* Based on size and charge
* **Zone electrophoresis** - inert material used between electrodes
* **Moving boundary electrophoresis** - in solution without supporting media

### Chromatography
* Separate based on size, affinity and charge
* Sample loaded onto stationary phase
* Separation while moving with mobile phase

### Immunoassays
* Antibody-based analytical methods
* **Highly specific** - little cross-reactivity
* **High sensitivity** - detection at low concentrations
* Radio-immunoassay (RIA)
* Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA)
* Fluoro-immunoassay (FIA)
* Chemiluminescence immunoassay (CLIA)

### Quality control
* Accuracy
* Precision
* Patient safety and effective healthcare delivery
* Compliance with Standards and Regulations
* External quality assurance - consistent performance within laboratory compared to other laboratories
